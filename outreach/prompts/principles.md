# outreach/prompts/principles.md — Voice & Craft Rules

> Every subagent that writes outreach content must read this file before drafting. These rules define what a good message looks like, what's forbidden, and how Varun's voice should come through. When in doubt, re-read `profile.md` section 17 for tone calibration.

---

## 1. FORBIDDEN WORDS & PHRASES

Never use any of the following in a subject line or message body. No exceptions, no "just this once."

**Banned phrases:**
- "synergy" / "synergies"
- "leverage" (as a verb meaning "use")
- "passionate"
- "huge fan"
- "I came across your profile"
- "I hope this finds you well"
- "circling back"
- "touching base"
- "wanted to reach out"
- "quick question"

**Also banned (from `profile.md` section 18):**
- "best-in-class"
- Any buzzword soup that a human wouldn't say out loud to a friend

**Test:** read the sentence aloud. If it sounds like a LinkedIn automation template, rewrite it.

---

## 2. MESSAGE STRUCTURE

Every outreach message follows four parts. Max **6 sentences total** across all four.

| Part | Job | Sentences |
|---|---|---|
| **Hook** | Earn attention. Reference something specific about the recipient or their company. | 1–2 |
| **Bridge** | Connect their context to Varun's relevant experience. No life story — one concrete fact. | 1–2 |
| **Ask** | State what you want. Be specific and low-commitment. | 1 |
| **Close** | Make it easy to say yes. Propose a concrete next step. | 1 |

**Hard ceiling:** 6 sentences. If the draft is longer, cut. Shorter is fine — 4 sentences is often better than 6.

---

## 3. SUBJECT LINE RULES

- **Length:** 4–6 words.
- **No questions.** Statements only.
- **No ALL CAPS.** Sentence case or lower case.
- **No emojis.**
- **Never contains the recipient's name.** (Looks automated.)
- **Never starts with "Re:"** unless it is actually a reply to an existing thread.
- **Never starts with "Quick"** or "Hey" — these are spam signals.

Good: `Growth at seed-stage edtech`
Good: `Founding team after Series A`
Good: `PM role, similar build`
Bad: `Quick question about your role`
Bad: `Hey Priya - exciting opportunity!`
Bad: `RE: Following up`

---

## 4. HOOK QUALITY BAR

The hook must reference something **specific** and **recent** (within the last 60 days) about the recipient or their company. It must be verifiable from a public source.

**Acceptable hook triggers:**
- A LinkedIn post or article they wrote
- A product launch or feature ship
- A new hire announcement (especially if it signals team growth)
- A fundraise (seed, Series A, etc.)
- A podcast appearance or conference talk
- A public blog post or changelog entry

**Never acceptable:**
- Generic praise of the company ("I love what you're building")
- Compliments about the recipient's career trajectory
- Anything that could apply to 50 other people without changing a word

**Test:** if you delete the company name and the hook still makes sense, it's too generic. Rewrite.

---

## 5. VOICE

Write as Varun — direct, one human to another. Not as a bot, not as a recruiter, not as a salesperson.

**Voice principles (derived from `profile.md` section 17):**
- **Confident, not arrogant.** State facts. Don't hedge ("I think I might be a good fit"). Don't oversell ("I would be perfect for this").
- **Specific, not vague.** Numbers over adjectives. "12x user growth in one month" over "significant growth experience."
- **Brief, not curt.** Every sentence earns its place. Cut any sentence that doesn't add information.
- **Peer-to-peer.** Write like you're emailing someone you'd work alongside, not someone you're pitching up to.
- **Honest about status.** Varun is between roles after building Subatom and Ellyn. Don't hide it. Don't spin it. The builder context is the strength.

**Tone test:** if the message could have been written by a career coach, it's wrong. If it sounds like something Varun would actually type in a Slack DM to a founder he respects, it's right.

## 6. GOOD EXAMPLES

### A. Founding-team role at a seed-stage SaaS

**Subject:** `Founding operator after seed raise`

> Saw your seed announcement last week — building invoice automation for Indian SMEs is a sharp wedge. I spent the last year building Subatom (job automation platform) end-to-end: product logic, system design, rapid experiments. Before that I grew a student platform to 100K+ users with 12x growth in a single month at Uncover Campus. I'm looking for a founding team where I can own a function from zero. Worth a 15 min call next week?

*Why it works: specific hook (seed raise + product wedge), two concrete proof points with numbers, clear ask, 5 sentences.*

### B. Growth lead at an edtech post-raise

**Subject:** `Growth after Series A edtech`

> Your Series A post mentioned scaling school partnerships across Tier-2 cities — that's a distribution problem I've solved before. At Uncover Campus I built a GTM engine across 72+ college societies and hit 100K students, with 12x growth in one month during our push into new cities. I've also done the VC side at Eximius Ventures, so I know what board-ready growth metrics look like. Happy to share what worked and what didn't over a 15 min call.

*Why it works: hooks on a specific post detail (Tier-2 expansion), bridges with directly relevant experience, offers value (not just asking), 4 sentences.*

### C. PM role at a B2B fintech

**Subject:** `PM with fintech build experience`

> Noticed you shipped the UPI reconciliation feature last month — that's a hard problem for SME customers. I built two products end-to-end (Subatom for job automation, Ellyn for AI email discovery) and spent 13 months at Eximius Ventures screening fintech deals, so I think about product through both a builder and investor lens. Looking for a PM role where I own a surface area and the metrics behind it. Worth a short call to see if there's fit?

*Why it works: hooks on a specific recent product ship, bridges operator + investor lens (differentiator), honest about what he wants, 4 sentences.*

## 7. BAD EXAMPLES

### A. Generic flattery opener

**Subject:** `Excited about your company`

> Hi! I'm a huge fan of what your team is building. I came across your profile on LinkedIn and I'd love to connect. I have experience in growth, product, and strategy and I think I could add a lot of value to your team. Let me know if you'd be open to a chat!

*What's wrong: no specific hook, three banned phrases ("huge fan", "I came across your profile", "let me know"), no concrete proof point, could be sent to anyone.*

### B. Wall-of-text with buzzwords

**Subject:** `Quick question about leveraging growth synergies`

> I hope this finds you well. I wanted to reach out because I'm passionate about leveraging best-in-class growth strategies to drive synergies across product and go-to-market functions. I've worked across multiple domains including VC, consulting, product, growth, and operations, and I believe my cross-functional experience could be a great fit for your organisation. I've led teams of 100+, driven 12x growth, built AI products, screened 100+ deals, and generated significant revenue impact. I'd love to set up a time to discuss how my background could complement your team's goals. Looking forward to hearing from you.

*What's wrong: six banned phrases in one message, reads like a LinkedIn bot, buries real achievements in a wall of buzzwords, no specific hook about the recipient, 6 sentences but zero information density.*

### C. Fake familiarity / mutual connection claim

**Subject:** `Re: Our conversation at the summit`

> Great running into you at the SaaS founders meetup last Thursday! As I mentioned, I've been building in the job automation space and would love to explore how I could contribute to your team. I think we have a lot of mutual connections in the Delhi startup ecosystem. Let me know what works for you and we can grab coffee.

*What's wrong: fabricated prior interaction (violates GUARDRAILS.md section 1.7), fake "Re:" subject, claims mutual connections that may not exist, no verifiable hook, vague ask.*

---

## 8. CLOSING LINE PREFERENCES

**Preferred:**
- "Worth a 15 min call next week?"

**Acceptable alternatives:**
- "Happy to share more over a short call."
- "Open to a 15 min chat [specific day range]?"
- "Worth a quick call to see if there's fit?"

**Never use:**
- "Let me know what works for you" — too open-ended, easy to ignore
- "Looking forward to hearing from you" — passive, assumes interest
- "Would love to connect" — vague, no commitment asked
- "I'm available anytime" — signals low value

**Principle:** the close should make it easy to say yes. Propose a specific, low-commitment action. "15 min call next week" is concrete. "Let me know" is not.
